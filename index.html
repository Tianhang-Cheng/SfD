3D metrics — mesh vs. Blender ground truth

Marching cubes on the canonical SDF of the Mat checkpoint, replicated to every instance and compared against the mesh exported straight out of the .blend (scripts/eval_mesh_3d.py, 200k surface samples per mesh, no ICP — the alignment is the analytic SfM↔Blender similarity of utils/blender_align.py). Distances are given as a percentage of the ground-truth bounding box diagonal, since the objects differ in metric size by more than 10×. Pose spread is how far the per-instance canonical→Blender transforms disagree with each other, in the same units: it is an upper bound on how much of the distance is SfM pose error rather than shape error (it is measured at the corners of the canonical unit cube, which is wider than the objects). At 9 objects with a mean CD-L1 of the same order as that spread, these distances say about as much about the poses as about the geometry. The 6 real-world captures (airplane, cake, cheese, cola, potato, yogurt) ship no .blend, so they have no 3D metrics at all.
